Alternating weeks: traditional intervals → Norwegian 4×4 → traditional → Norwegian. This gives you both pace-based and HR-based VO2max stimulus.

Norwegian 4×4 — How to Do It

  • Warmup: 2 mi easy jog
  • Rep 1: Run for 4 minutes at 90-95% max HR. For you that's 165-175 bpm. Don't look at pace — look at your heart rate. It'll probably land around 5:50-6:10/mi but the HR is what matters.
  • Recovery: Jog for 3 minutes at ~70% max HR (~128 bpm). Slow enough that HR actually comes down.
  • Repeat 3 more times (4 total).
  • Cooldown: 1.5 mi easy jog
  • Total time at VO2max zone: 16 minutes. That's the magic number.

Why HR instead of pace: On a tired day you'll run 6:10 and still hit 170 bpm. On a fresh day you'll run 5:50 at 170 bpm. The stimulus is always correct because your heart doesn't lie.

  • Weeks 6-9: 57 → 58-59. Intervals + Norwegian 4×4 kick in.
  • Weeks 10-12: 58 → 59-60 possible. Depends on consistency.
  • Check weekly, not daily. It lags behind real fitness by ~1-2 weeks.

Chicago Winter Running

Where to Run

  • Lakefront Trail — the go-to. 18 miles of paved path. Exposed to wind off the lake but well-maintained. Best for long runs and tempos.
  • 606 Trail — elevated, sheltered from wind, 2.7 mi out-and-back. Good for tempo repeats.
  • Lincoln Park / North Pond loop — ~3 mi loop, some wind protection from trees.
  • Treadmill — no shame. For intervals in December when it's 15°F and icy, the treadmill is smarter than risking a fall. Set 1% incline to simulate outdoor effort. Norwegian 4×4 works great on a treadmill (just set pace and watch HR).

Gear by Temperature

TempWhat to Wear
50-60°F (Oct)Shorts + long sleeve or T-shirt. You're fine.
40-50°F (Nov)Tights or shorts + long sleeve. Light gloves. Ear band optional.
25-40°F (Dec)Tights + long sleeve + wind-blocking vest or jacket. Gloves. Ear band or beanie. Buff/neck gaiter if windy.
Below 25°FFull tights + base layer + wind jacket. Mittens (warmer than gloves). Cover all skin. OR: treadmill.

Rule of thumb: Dress like it's 15-20°F warmer than the actual temp. You'll warm up after the first mile. If you're comfortable standing outside, you're overdressed for running.

Treadmill Guide

  • Set 1% incline for all runs — compensates for lack of wind resistance.
  • Strides on treadmill: At the end of your easy run, bump speed up by 2-3 mph every 5 sec until you hit 9.5-10.5 mph (~5:40-6:20 pace). Hold 10 sec. Step onto side rails, rest 30-45 sec. Repeat 4-6 times.
  • Norwegian 4×4 on treadmill: Works great. Set pace, watch HR on your watch. Adjust speed to stay in the 165-175 bpm zone. Step onto rails for recovery jog at 4.5-5.0 mph.
  • Hill sprints on treadmill: Set incline to 8-10%, speed to 9-10 mph. Sprint 10-12 sec. Grab rails, step onto sides. Rest 60-90 sec. Repeat.
  • Tempo on treadmill: Set your tempo pace (e.g. 9.2 mph = 6:31/mi) and hold it. Treadmill tempos are actually easier to pace than outdoor since you can't drift.

Cold Weather Tips

  • Wind: Run INTO the wind on the way out, wind at your back coming home. You don't want to be sweaty and facing a headwind on the way back.
  • Ice: If there's ice on the ground, treadmill. A slip = weeks of missed training. Not worth it.
  • Breathing: Cold air can burn your lungs. A buff/gaiter over your mouth warms the air. Breathe through it.
  • Warm up inside: Do your dynamic warmup (leg swings, lunges) in your apartment before going out. Start running immediately — don't stand around in the cold.
  • Shorter intervals in extreme cold: If it's below 20°F and you insist on running outside, swap 4×1000m for 6×400m. Less time exposed at hard effort.

Rules

  • Easy runs = truly easy. 8:00-8:45/mi. This is the #1 rule. If every run feels hard, you're not recovering and VO2max won't improve.
  • Max 10% mileage increase per week. If you miss a week, drop back one week in the plan.
  • 2+ days of pain = extra rest day. Missing 1 run > missing 3 weeks with an injury.
  • Sleep 7.5-8 hrs. VO2max adaptations happen while you sleep. Non-negotiable.
  • Eat 2,800-3,200 cal on workout days. You're running 30 mi/week now. Fuel it.
  • Gym legs every other Tuesday. Upper body whenever you want (Tue/Thu).
  • Bodyweight strength circuit from the 5K plan — keep doing it Friday after easy runs.
  • If you find a race during the plan, do a 10-day mini-taper (drop volume 40%, keep 1 sharpener) and race it. Then resume where you left off.

After Week 12

If you've hit the benchmarks, you're ready to race sub-19 and sub-40 with a 2-week taper. If not, repeat Weeks 8-12 at the same paces — the volume itself will push your fitness forward. You can also extend indefinitely by repeating Phase 2 and increasing mileage by 1-2 mi/week.

The work compounds. Consistency over 12 weeks beats any single workout.
