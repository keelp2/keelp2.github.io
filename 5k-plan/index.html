If Things Go Wrong

Training Issues

  • Workout feels terrible: Cut short. 3 good reps > 5 bad reps.
  • Legs feel heavy/dead: Take an extra rest day. Freshness > fitness.
  • Minor ache: Ice + foam roll + reduce next run. Skip if 2+ days.
  • Sick: No running with fever. Mild cold = easy runs only.
  • Hungover Sunday: Walk 30 min + foam roll + 100 oz water. Easy 3 mi only if okay by afternoon.

Race Day Issues

  • Bad weather: Adjust goal by 15-30 sec. Don't fight headwind/rain.
  • Go out too fast mile 1: Don't panic. Settle to 6:10-6:15 in mile 2, re-engage at 2.5.
  • Side stitch: Slow slightly, exhale forcefully on left foot strike, press on the stitch.
  • Legs heavy early: Stay at 6:10-6:15 through mile 2, then reassess. You often feel better after 10 min.

Running Glossary

Plain-English explanations of every term in this plan. Bookmark this tab.

Workout Types

  • Intervals / Repeats (e.g. "5×800m"): Run a set distance fast, then jog slowly to recover, then repeat. "5×800m" means: run 800 meters hard (about 2 laps of a track), jog for 2 minutes to catch your breath, then do it again — 5 times total. These build your engine.
  • Tempo run (e.g. "2.5 mi at 6:15"): A sustained run at a "comfortably hard" pace — you can talk in short phrases but not full sentences. Faster than your easy pace, slower than a sprint. Builds endurance at speed.
  • Easy run: A relaxed jog where you could hold a full conversation. Should feel genuinely easy — not pushing at all. This is recovery and aerobic base. Most of your runs should feel like this.
  • Strides (e.g. "6×100m strides"): Short, smooth accelerations — NOT sprints. Find a flat stretch of about 100 meters (a football field). Gradually speed up to about 90% effort over the first half, hold it, then gradually slow down. Walk back to where you started. That's one stride. They teach your legs to move fast without the fatigue of a full workout. Think of them as "reminding your legs what fast feels like."
  • Sharpener: A very short, light speed session done a few days before the race. Just 2-3 short fast efforts to keep your legs feeling quick without creating any fatigue. Like revving the engine without driving anywhere.

Distances

  • 100m: The length of a football field (straightaway). Takes about 15-20 seconds.
  • 200m: Half a lap around a standard track. Takes about 40-45 seconds at this plan's paces.
  • 400m: One full lap around a standard track. Takes about 1:25-1:30 at this plan's paces.
  • 800m: Two laps around a track (half a mile). Takes about 2:55-3:00 at this plan's paces.
  • 1000m (1K): 2.5 laps around a track (0.62 miles). Takes about 3:40-3:45 at this plan's paces.
  • 5K: 3.1 miles. Your race distance.
  • No track nearby? Use your phone's GPS or a running watch. Or measure on Google Maps. A long block in a city grid is usually about 200m.

Pace & Effort Terms

  • Pace (e.g. "6:07/mi"): How many minutes it takes you to run one mile. 6:07/mi means each mile takes 6 minutes and 7 seconds. Lower number = faster.
  • Race pace: The pace you plan to hold during the race. For sub-19, that's 6:07/mi.
  • Recovery / jog recovery: After a fast repeat, jog very slowly (or even walk) for the specified time. This lets your heart rate come down before the next hard effort. Don't skip it — the recovery is part of the workout.
  • WU / CD: Warmup / Cooldown. WU = easy jogging before the hard part to get blood flowing. CD = easy jogging after to bring your heart rate down gradually.
  • Negative split: Running the second half of a race faster than the first half. This is the goal — start controlled, finish strong.
  • RPE (effort): How hard it feels on a 1-10 scale. Easy run = 4-5. Tempo = 7. Intervals = 8-9. Race = 9-10.

Recovery & Body Maintenance

  • Foam rolling: Using a foam cylinder to massage your muscles. Lay the roller on the ground, put the muscle on top of it, and slowly roll back and forth using your body weight. It hurts a bit (in a good way). Spend about 30-60 seconds on each muscle group: front of thighs (quads), outside of thighs (IT band), calves, and butt (glutes). A $20 foam roller from Amazon works fine.
  • IT band: A thick band of tissue that runs along the outside of your thigh from your hip to your knee. It gets tight from running. Foam roll the outside of your thigh.
  • Hip flexor stretch: The muscles at the front of your hip that get tight from sitting all day. To stretch: kneel on one knee (like you're proposing), push your hips forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the kneeling leg's hip. Hold 90 seconds each side.
  • Taper: Intentionally reducing your training volume in the 1-2 weeks before a race so your body can fully recover and arrive at race day fresh and fast. You'll feel restless and antsy — that means it's working. Trust the process.
  • EPOC: "Afterburn" — your body keeps burning extra calories for hours after a hard workout while it recovers. This is why key session days have higher calorie targets.

Nutrition Terms

  • BMR: Basal Metabolic Rate — calories your body burns just being alive (breathing, heartbeat, etc.) even if you lay in bed all day.
  • TDEE: Total Daily Energy Expenditure — BMR plus the calories burned from daily activity (walking, working, etc.). This is your "maintenance" calories.
  • Carbs / carb loading: Carbohydrates are your body's preferred fuel for fast running. Pasta, rice, bread, bananas, oatmeal. "Carb loading" just means eating extra carbs the day or two before a race to make sure your fuel tank is full. For a 5K you don't need to go crazy — just have pasta for dinner.
  • Glycogen: Stored carbs in your muscles and liver. When you eat carbs, your body converts them to glycogen for fuel. You want these stores topped off on race day.
  • Protein: Repairs muscles after training. Chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, protein shakes. Aim for some protein at every meal.
  • Macros: Short for macronutrients — the three main categories of food: carbs, protein, and fat. The targets in this plan tell you roughly how many grams of each to aim for.

How to Read Workout Notation

  • "5×800m at 2:55-3:00 w/ 2:00 jog recovery"
  • → Run 800 meters (2 track laps) in 2 minutes 55 seconds to 3 minutes
  • → Then jog very slowly for 2 minutes
  • → Repeat 5 times total
  • → Do a 1.5 mile easy warmup jog before and 1 mile cooldown jog after
  • "1mi WU > 1mi at 6:07 > 3:00 jog > 1mi at 6:07 > CD"
  • → Jog 1 mile easy (warmup)
  • → Run 1 mile in 6:07 (hard)
  • → Jog for 3 minutes (recovery)
  • → Run 1 mile in 6:07 again (hard)
  • → Jog 1 mile easy (cooldown)
