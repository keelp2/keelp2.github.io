Hill Sprints — What Are These?

Find a steep-ish hill (6-10% grade). Sprint ALL OUT up it for 10-12 seconds. Walk back down. Repeat 6 times.

  • Why: Builds raw power and explosiveness with almost zero injury risk (short duration, soft effort on the downhill walk). Recruits fast-twitch muscle fibers that easy running ignores.
  • How hard: 100% effort. Pump your arms. Drive your knees. 10 seconds and done.
  • Recovery: Walk down slowly. Full recovery (60-90 sec) before the next one.
  • You won't be sore because it's so short. But over 8 weeks, the power gains compound.

Week 7 Key Workouts

  • Mon — 6×800m at 2:48-2:53 (5:38-5:48/mi) w/ 90sec recovery. Faster than Phase 2. Shorter rest. This is the peak VO2max session.
  • Wed — 3 mi tempo at 6:15 + 4×400m at 1:22-1:25 (5:30-5:40/mi). Combines endurance and kick. Like a mini race simulation.

Week 8 Key Workouts

  • Mon — 2×1.5mi at 6:07-6:12 w/ 3:00 jog. Goal race pace for extended reps. If you can hold 6:07 for 1.5 miles twice, sub-19 5K is locked in.
  • Wed — Optional Time Trial: 2-mile all-out. Warmup 2mi → race 2 miles → cooldown. Target: under 12:00 (6:00/mi). This predicts ~18:45-18:55 5K and ~38:30-39:30 10K.

Garmin VO2max Expectations

  • Weeks 1-3: May not move. Base building doesn't spike VO2max immediately.
  • Weeks 4-6: Should see 56 → 57-58. The intervals + longer runs kick in.
  • Weeks 7-8: 58-60 is possible if everything clicks. More likely 57-59.
  • Don't obsess over the Garmin number daily — check it weekly. It lags behind actual fitness.

Rules

  • Easy runs = truly easy. 8:00-8:45/mi. If you can't hold a conversation, slow down. This is the #1 mistake — making easy days too hard kills your recovery for Monday/Wednesday.
  • Never increase mileage more than 10% per week. If you miss a week, don't jump back to where you were. Drop back one week.
  • If something hurts for 2+ days, take an extra rest day. Missing one run is nothing. Missing 3 weeks with a stress fracture kills the whole block.
  • Sleep 7.5-8 hrs. Still the #1 recovery tool. VO2max adaptations happen while you sleep.
  • Eat 2,800-3,200 cal on workout days. You're running more now. Don't undereat.
  • Upper body + abs on Tue/Thu = still fine. No heavy deadlifts.
  • Strength circuit from the 5K plan — keep doing it once a week (Friday after easy run).

What "Explosive and Fast" Means in Practice

Three things build explosiveness for a runner:

  • Hill sprints (Wed, Phase 1-2): Pure neuromuscular power. Recruits fast-twitch fibers. Makes you feel powerful even at easy pace.
  • Strides (Fri, every week): Teaches your legs to move fast without fatigue. Keeps your turnover quick.
  • Short fast reps — 200m/400m (Phase 2-3): Top-end speed. The difference between "hanging on" at 6:07 and "choosing to run" 6:07.

By Week 8, your easy pace will feel faster, your intervals will be at paces you couldn't touch in September, and your kick will be even more dangerous than the 1:17 you ran in the 5K plan.

When You're Ready to Race

After Week 8, you can either:

  • Race a 5K: Do a 2-week taper (same as the Oct 3 plan) and target sub-19.
  • Race a 10K: Do a 2-week taper with longer race-pace reps (2×2mi at 6:25). Target sub-40.
  • Keep building: Repeat Phase 2-3 at higher paces. The engine keeps growing.

The work you did in September isn't wasted — this plan stacks directly on top of it.

Start date: whenever you're ready. Week 1, Day 1 = your next Monday.
